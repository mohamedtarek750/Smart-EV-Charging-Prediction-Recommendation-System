01  ·  The data
No public dataset exists — so the city was simulated
A discrete-event simulator — not random numbers. Every car is an event with an arrival time, a queue and a charging session.
  • Eight venue types, each with its own hourly demand shape — government, business, residential, retail, transit, leisure, hotel, education
  • Friday–Saturday weekend and 17 Egyptian public holidays
  • Charging physics — pack size, state of charge, power taper above 80 %, thermal penalty in cold and heat
  • M/M/c queue with abandonment — 6.9 % of drivers give up and leave
  • Weather, venue events, +45 % adoption growth across the year

03  ·  The decision
Forecasting says what will happen.
Optimisation decides what to do about it.
Once you know 30 cars arrive between 17:00 and 19:00, powering them is not a prediction problem — it is constrained allocation. So it is solved as a linear program, not another regressor.
minimise   Σ price[t]·x[v,t]   energy bill
            + w · P_peak          flatten the profile
            + 500 · Σ u[v]       unmet energy

s.t.     Σ x[v,t] + u[v] = E_v   every car gets its charge
         0 ≤ x[v,t] ≤ P_v       charger + vehicle limit
         x = 0 outside [arrive, leave]
         Σ_v x[v,t] ≤ cap[t]        grid ceiling
Solved with HiGHS via scipy.optimize.linprog. 934 vehicles · 32,616 kWh requested. The unmet-energy slack keeps it always feasible.
